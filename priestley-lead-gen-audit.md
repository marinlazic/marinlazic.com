# Marin Lazic Portfolio Site: Lead Generation Audit

**Through the Lens of Daniel Priestley**

*Frameworks applied: Oversubscribed, Key Person of Influence, 24 Assets, Scorecard Marketing, 7-11-4 Rule*

---

## The Diagnosis

Marin, your site has personality. It has voice. It has credibility. What it does not have is a lead generation engine. Right now, your website is a digital business card. It tells people who you are and waits for them to reach out. That is the equivalent of standing in a room full of ideal clients and hoping someone walks up to you. Priestley would say you are leaving thousands of dollars on the table because you have not built the **assets** that do the selling while you sleep. [1]

Let me break this down using his frameworks.

---

## 1. The 7-11-4 Problem

Priestley's research, backed by Google's Zero Moment of Truth data, shows that a stranger needs **7 hours of content consumption**, across **11 interactions**, on **4 different platforms** before they trust you enough to buy. [2] [3]

Your current site offers roughly **3 minutes** of content. One platform. One interaction. Then a contact form. You are asking someone to go from stranger to "give me your email" in under 180 seconds. That is like proposing marriage on the first date.

| 7-11-4 Metric | What Priestley Recommends | What Your Site Delivers | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7 hours** of content | Long-form articles, videos, podcasts, case studies, downloadable guides | ~3 min read, no video, no downloads | 6 hrs 57 min |
| **11 interactions** | Email sequences, social touchpoints, quizzes, events, webinars | 1 page visit + 1 form submission | 9 interactions |
| **4 platforms** | Website, email, social media, events/webinars | Website only | 3 platforms |

**What to build:**

**A. Add a video introduction.** Even 90 seconds of you talking to camera on the hero section would triple the time spent on page. Your voice and energy are your biggest assets. The written copy is great, but Priestley is clear: video builds trust faster than any other medium. [2]

**B. Create a content hub.** Add a "Resources" or "Insights" section with 3 to 5 articles. Topics that write themselves from your existing knowledge: "Why Your HRV Matters More Than Your Bench Press," "The Jet Lag Protocol We Used at Paris 2024," "What Corporate Athletes Can Learn from Water Polo." Each article is another hour of content consumption, another interaction, another reason to come back.

**C. Build an email nurture sequence.** Right now your form says "No spam. No 47-email nurture sequence." That is funny copy. But Priestley would tell you that a well-crafted 5 to 7 email sequence is not spam. It is the mechanism that takes someone from "interested" to "ready to buy." You need those 11 touchpoints. An email sequence is the most efficient way to deliver them. [3]

---

## 2. The Scorecard Lead Magnet (Your Biggest Missing Asset)

Priestley co-founded ScoreApp because he believes the **interactive scorecard** is the single most powerful lead generation tool in business. [4] A scorecard is a short quiz or assessment that gives the prospect a personalised score and you a qualified lead with data about their pain points.

Your current lead magnet is... nothing. You have a contact form that says "drop your email." That is a cold ask with zero value exchange. Priestley's rule: **give value before you ask for anything.** [1]

**What to build:**

**"The Stoke Tank Scorecard"** — a 2-minute assessment that scores visitors across 5 dimensions:

| Dimension | Sample Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleep Quality** | "How many hours of uninterrupted sleep do you average per night?" | Identifies the #1 performance killer |
| **Stress Management** | "When was the last time you took a full day off without checking email?" | Maps to your origin story about stress |
| **Recovery Protocols** | "Do you have a structured recovery routine after intense work periods?" | Qualifies them for your services |
| **Energy Management** | "At what time of day does your energy typically crash?" | Shows you understand their daily reality |
| **Movement & Training** | "How many structured training sessions do you complete per week?" | Gauges their current baseline |

At the end, they get a personalised "Stoke Tank Score" out of 100 with a brief breakdown. You get their email, their score, and data on exactly where they are struggling. Now your follow-up email is not generic. It is: "Hey, I noticed your sleep score was 3 out of 10. Here is the one thing I would change first." That is how you convert at 40 to 60% instead of 5 to 10%. [4]

This scorecard replaces your current contact form as the primary CTA across the entire site.

## 3. The Oversubscribed Campaign Structure

Priestley's five-phase campaign model is designed to create **supply-demand tension**, the force that makes people line up to buy. [5] Right now, your site has zero scarcity, zero urgency, and unlimited availability. You are positioned as "available whenever." Priestley would say that is the fastest way to be perceived as low value.

> "People don't buy what others want to sell. They buy what others want to buy." — Daniel Priestley, *Oversubscribed* [5]

**What to build:**

**A. Capacity signalling.** Add a small, honest indicator somewhere on the Services section: "Currently accepting 3 new 1:1 clients for Q2 2026." This is not fake scarcity. You genuinely have limited capacity. Priestley says you should **signal** that capacity publicly so people understand the constraint is real. [5]

**B. A waiting list mechanism.** When those 3 spots fill, the CTA changes to "Join the Waiting List for Q3." This creates the supply-demand tension that Priestley considers the single most important driver of premium pricing. People on a waiting list are warmer leads than anyone who fills out a generic contact form. [5]

**C. Social proof as campaign fuel.** Priestley's campaign model says you should "share success stories publicly" to create aspirational pull. [5] Your testimonials section currently has placeholder quotes. Replace them with real client stories. Even better, add short video testimonials. One genuine 30-second clip from an athlete or executive saying "Marin changed how I operate" is worth more than 10 written quotes.

## 4. The Key Person of Influence Gap

Priestley's KPI framework has five steps: **Pitch, Publish, Product, Profile, Partnership.** [6] Let me score you:

| KPI Step | Status | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Pitch** | Strong | Your "stoke tank" framing is distinctive and memorable. The origin story about getting dropped is compelling. You have a clear, differentiated message. |
| **Publish** | Weak | You have no published content on this site. No articles, no downloadable guide, no podcast, no video. Publishing is how you build the 7 hours of content needed for the 7-11-4 rule. |
| **Product** | Moderate | You have four service offerings, but they are described, not productised. Priestley would want to see a named methodology with a clear process. |
| **Profile** | Moderate | Paris 2024, World Championships, and the Australian team are strong credentials. But they are buried in the About section. They should be front and centre as trust signals. |
| **Partnership** | Missing | No visible partnerships, collaborations, or associations. Where is Water Polo Australia? Where is the Australian Olympic Committee? Where is Otion? Logos and associations build instant credibility. [6] |

**What to build:**

**A. Productise your methodology.** Give your coaching framework a name. Not just "1:1 Executive Performance Coaching." Something like "The Stoke System" or "The Performance Biology Method." A named methodology is an intellectual property asset that Priestley considers foundational to business value. [1] It makes you referable. People say "Have you done Marin's Stoke System?" instead of "Have you tried this coach?"

**B. Add a trust bar.** Below the hero section, add a horizontal strip with logos: Australian Olympic Committee, Water Polo Australia, TRX, Otion. Priestley calls these **positioning assets** (awards, accreditations, associations, acknowledgments). [1] They answer the unconscious question every visitor has: "Why should I trust this person?"

**C. Publish one signature piece of content.** A downloadable PDF guide, a video masterclass, or a long-form article. Something that demonstrates your expertise and gives people a reason to share your name. Priestley says publishing is the fastest path to becoming a Key Person of Influence in your industry. [6]

## 5. The LAPS Funnel Breakdown

Priestley's sales system follows four stages: **Lead, Appointment, Presentation, Sale.** [1] Your current site skips straight from Lead to Sale. There is no middle.

| LAPS Stage | Current State | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead** | Contact form with no value exchange | Replace with Stoke Tank Scorecard. Add content hub for organic leads. |
| **Appointment** | No mechanism to book a call | Add a Calendly embed or "Book a Discovery Call" button directly on the site |
| **Presentation** | No structured presentation of your offer | Create a "How It Works" section showing your 3 or 4 step process with clear outcomes |
| **Sale** | No pricing signals, no packages, no next steps | Add capacity indicators and a clear "What Happens Next" after someone books a call |

**The critical missing piece is the Appointment stage.** Someone fills out your form and then... what? They wait for you to email them back? Priestley would say that is where you lose 60% of warm leads. They filled out the form while motivated. By the time you reply 12 hours later, they have moved on. [1]

**Add a Calendly or booking link** that appears immediately after form submission: "Thanks for reaching out. Want to skip the back-and-forth? Book a 15-minute discovery call now." This alone could double your conversion rate from lead to appointment.

## 6. Quick Wins (Implement This Week)

These are changes that require minimal effort but would have immediate impact on lead generation:

**1. Replace "Let's Go" CTA with "Take the Stoke Tank Assessment."** A quiz is more compelling than a generic contact button. It promises value before asking for commitment.

**2. Add a sticky header CTA.** As visitors scroll, keep a small "Check Your Stoke Tank Score" button visible in the navigation. Right now, the only CTA is at the very bottom. Most visitors never scroll that far.

**3. Add credential logos below the hero.** Water Polo Australia, Australian Olympic Committee, TRX, Otion. Five seconds of visual trust.

**4. Add a "Book a Call" button to every service card.** Each service description ends with "What Actually Happens" but no way to take action. Add a direct booking link.

**5. Replace placeholder testimonials immediately.** Fake testimonials are worse than no testimonials. Even two real quotes from athletes or clients would be more powerful than four fabricated ones.

**6. Add an exit-intent popup with the scorecard.** When someone moves their cursor to leave the page, offer the Stoke Tank Assessment. This captures leads who were interested but not ready to fill out a form.

---

## The Bottom Line

Your site is a 9 out of 10 for personality and a 3 out of 10 for lead generation. The voice is distinctive, the credentials are real, and the origin story is compelling. But you are treating your website like a brochure when it should be a **lead generation machine**.

Priestley's entire philosophy comes down to one idea: **income follows assets.** [1] Right now, your site has one asset (the contact form) doing the work of what should be six or seven (scorecard, content hub, email sequence, booking system, trust signals, capacity indicators, social proof).

Build the Stoke Tank Scorecard first. It is the highest-leverage change you can make. Everything else flows from there.
