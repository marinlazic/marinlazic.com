# Core: no off season

Idea, in one sentence
You don't get an off season, so the plan has to fit inside your week.

The claim
Most training plans for busy people are built for the best week of the year. That is why they fail by week three. Build the plan for your worst week and add to it, and you will still be training in December.

Why it is true
Training is a stress. The body does not get fitter during the session. It gets fitter afterwards, while it repairs. The gain comes from repeating that loop for months, not from one big week. A plan you can only keep in a quiet week breaks the loop every time work gets loud. A smaller plan you never break keeps it going.

The proof on record
Athletes get a season. Build, peak, recover, to a plan. Four campaigns with the Australian men's water polo team since 2023, each one built, peaked and recovered on purpose. Nobody trained flat out all year. A founder has no season. The year is one long block, so the structure has to live inside the week instead.

[MARIN: add a real example] A client who kept two sessions through a raise, a launch or a board quarter, and where they were six months later. Or your own week when the team is away.

What the reader should do this week
Look at your worst week this year. Count the sessions you still did. That number is your floor. Put that many sessions in the diary as fixed, and treat anything above it as a bonus. Then book one easy week before your next heavy quarter, the same way a team books recovery before a tournament.

The objection
"Two sessions a week will not get me fit."
Two sessions you keep for a year beat five you keep for three weeks. The gain comes from the repeat.
