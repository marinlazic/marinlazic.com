Last week the team finished a block. Volume came down, the sharp work went up, and then they stopped. Not because anyone was tired. Because the plan said so. The rest was in the diary before the block began.

That is how a season works. Build, peak, recover. We have run four of them since 2023 and every one has had the same shape. Load goes up in steps. Easy weeks are planned in. Nobody trains flat out all year, because flat out all year is how you arrive at the tournament fried.

You do not have a season. I know this because every founder I talk to describes their year the same way. Q4 runs into January. The raise runs into the hire. There is no tournament to peak for and no end of season to recover in. There is a year, and it is all heavy.

So when you build a training plan, you build it the only way you can see: around the week you are in. Which is usually a quiet one, because that is when people have the energy to make plans. Five sessions. Early starts. It works for three weeks. Then a deal lands or a flight does, you miss two sessions, then four, and by the end of the month the plan is something you used to do.

The plan was not too hard. It was built for your best week. You get a handful of those a year.

## The gain comes from the repeat

Training is a stress. You do not get fitter during the session. You get fitter afterwards, while the body repairs what you did and builds a bit extra so the next one is easier. Work, then rest, then adapt. Then go again from a slightly higher base.

That loop only pays if it keeps running. One big week and three quiet ones does less than four modest weeks in a row. The modest weeks keep the loop going. The big week breaks it. This is the whole reason a season has easy weeks in it. We are protecting the repeat.

You cannot protect it with a season. So protect it inside the week.

Think back to the worst week you have had this year. Travel, deadline, sick kid, all of it. How many sessions did you still do? One, maybe two. That number is your floor. It is what your life can carry when everything goes wrong, and it is what the plan should be built on.

Put that many sessions in the diary as fixed. Strength first, because it is the one that holds everything else up. Then something that gets your heart rate up for 30 or 40 minutes. Pick the days you are most likely to be home and the time you are least likely to be interrupted.

Everything above the floor is a bonus. In a quiet week, add one. In a loud week, drop back and feel nothing about it. You did not fail the plan. The plan was built for this.

One more thing from the team. We book the easy week before the hard block, not after we feel the fatigue. Once you feel it, the choice has been made for you. Look at the next quarter, find the heaviest fortnight, and put an easy training week in front of it. Keep the floor, drop the weights by a third, go to bed early.
