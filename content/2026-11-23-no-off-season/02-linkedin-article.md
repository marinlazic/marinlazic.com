# Build your training plan for your worst week

Athletes get an off season. You do not. That one difference explains why most training plans for founders and executives are gone by week three, and it tells you how to build one that lasts the year.

I coach strength and conditioning for the Australian Men's Water Polo Team. Since 2023 we have run four campaigns, including Paris 2024, and every one has followed the same shape. Build, peak, recover. Load goes up in steps with easy weeks planned in. Near the tournament, volume drops and sharpness goes up. After it, the players rest, and the rest is in the plan before the season starts. Nobody guesses and nobody trains flat out all year.

Then I sit down with a founder, and none of that applies. There is no tournament to peak for and no end of season to recover in. There is a year, and it is all heavy.

## The plan fails because it was built for the wrong week

Here is what usually happens. You decide to get serious. You build a plan around five sessions a week, which you can do, because you built it on a Sunday night in a quiet month. For three weeks it works. Then a deal closes, or a hire falls through, or you fly. You miss two sessions, then four, and by the end of the month the plan is a thing you used to do.

The plan was not too hard. It was built for your best week, and you only get a few of those a year.

## The gain comes from the repeat, not the week

Training is a stress. You do not get fitter during the session. You get fitter afterwards, while the body repairs the damage and builds a bit extra so the next session is easier. Work, then rest, then adapt. Then go again from a slightly higher base.

That loop only pays if it keeps running. One big week followed by three quiet ones does less than four modest weeks in a row, because the modest weeks keep the loop going and the big week breaks it. This is the whole reason a team's season has easy weeks built in. We are protecting the repeat.

You do not have a season to protect it with. So you have to protect it inside the week.

## Find your floor and build on it

Look back at the worst week you have had this year. The one with the travel, the deadline and the sick child. Count the training sessions you still did. Maybe it was two. Maybe it was one.

That number is your floor. It is the amount of training your life can carry when everything goes wrong, and it is the number the plan should be built on. Put that many sessions in the diary as fixed, on the days you are most likely to be at home, at the time you are least likely to be interrupted. Make them the sessions that matter most: a strength session, and something that gets your heart rate up for 30 to 40 minutes.

Anything above the floor is a bonus. When a quiet week comes, add a third session. When a loud week comes, drop back to the floor and feel nothing about it. You have not failed the plan. The plan was built for this.

## Book the easy week before you need it

Teams take an easy week before a tournament block and another after it. The timing is the point. We book it before the fatigue arrives, because once it arrives the choice has been made for you.

Do the same. Look at the next quarter and find the heaviest fortnight. Put an easy training week in front of it. Keep the floor, drop the weight by a third, and go to bed early. You will arrive at the heavy fortnight with something left, and you will still be training when it ends.

2. Put the floor in the diary as fixed sessions, strength first.
3. Decide now what you add in a quiet week, so the decision is made before the week arrives.
4. Find the heaviest fortnight in the next quarter and book an easy week before it.
