## 0:00 You don't get an off season

[Marin to camera, gym or pool deck. No music.]

You don't get an off season. And that one fact explains why your training plan is gone by week three, every time. By the end of this video you'll know how to build one that's still running in December.

## 0:50 The plan is built for the wrong week

Here's what usually happens. You decide to get serious about training. You sit down on a quiet Sunday and you build a plan. Five sessions a week. Early starts. And you can do it, because you built it in a quiet week.

For three weeks it works. Then a deal closes. Or a hire falls through. Or you're on a plane. You miss two sessions, then four. By the end of the month the plan is a thing you used to do.

[Cutaway: a diary with sessions crossed out, or a simple drawn week.]

And you tell yourself you weren't disciplined enough. That's not it. The plan wasn't too hard. It was built for your best week. And you get maybe a handful of those a year.

Compare that to what we do with the team. Every campaign since 2023 has had the same shape. Build, peak, recover. Load goes up in steps. Easy weeks are planned in before the hard ones. Near the tournament, volume drops and sharpness goes up. And then, after the tournament, they rest. The rest is in the plan before the season starts. Nobody guesses. Nobody trains flat out all year.

You don't have any of that. No tournament. No end of season. There's just the year, and it's all heavy.

## 2:20 The gain is in the repeat

So why does that matter so much? Because of how training actually works.

Training is a stress. You don't get fitter during the session. You get fitter afterwards, while your body repairs the damage and builds a little bit extra so the next session is easier.

[Cutaway: a line drawing of the training curve. The line dips after a session, then climbs past where it started.]

That line is your capacity. A session knocks it down. Rest brings it back, and a little past where it started. Work, then rest, then adapt. Then go again from a slightly higher base.

That loop only pays if it keeps running. One big week followed by three empty weeks does less than four modest weeks in a row. The modest weeks keep the loop going. The big week breaks it.

This is the whole reason a season has easy weeks in it. We're not being soft. We're protecting the repeat. You can't protect it with a season. So you have to protect it inside the week.

## 3:50 Find your floor

Here's how. Think back to the worst week you've had this year. The travel, the deadline, the sick kid, all at once. How many training sessions did you still do? Be honest. Maybe two. Maybe one.

That number is your floor. It's the amount of training your life can carry when everything goes wrong. And it's the number the plan should be built on.

[On screen: "Your floor" and the number 2.]

So put that many sessions in the diary as fixed. Pick the days you're most likely to be at home. Pick the time you're least likely to be interrupted. Make them the sessions that matter most. One strength session, because that holds everything else up. And one where your heart rate's up for 30 or 40 minutes.

Everything above the floor is a bonus. Quiet week? Add a third session. Loud week? Drop back to the floor and feel nothing about it. You haven't failed the plan. The plan was built for this.

[MARIN: tell the story of a client who kept two sessions through a heavy quarter, and where their training was six months later. Or your own week when the team is away and the diary is full.]

## 5:30 Book the easy week first

One more thing from the team, and it's the one most people skip.

We take an easy week before a tournament block. Not after we feel tired. Before. Because once you feel the fatigue, the choice has already been made for you.

[Cutaway: the season chart from the website, with the dips marked.]

So look at your next quarter. Find the heaviest fortnight. The board meeting, the launch, the trip. And put an easy training week right in front of it. Keep your floor. Drop the weights by about a third. Go to bed early. You'll arrive at the heavy fortnight with something left, and you'll still be training when it ends.

## 6:40 What to do this week

Three things.

One. Write down your worst week this year and how many sessions you kept. That's your floor.

Two. Put the floor in the diary as fixed sessions. Strength first.

Three. Find the heaviest fortnight in the next quarter and book an easy week before it.

Two sessions you keep for a year beat five you keep for three weeks.
