# LinkedIn post

Where it goes: the post box on Marin's LinkedIn profile. Text only, or text with one image.

Limits
- 3,000 characters is the hard limit. Aim for 900 to 1,500.
- Only the first 2 to 3 lines (about 210 characters) show before "see more". The first line must carry the claim on its own.
- No links in the body. LinkedIn shows link posts to fewer people. Put the call to action as plain words ("Take the free assessment at marinlazic.com") and put the full link in the first comment. Write that comment at the end of the file.
- No hashtags. No emoji. No tagging.

Shape
1. Hook: one line, under 12 words, the claim or the moment.
2. One blank line.
3. The story or the mechanism in short lines. One sentence per line, blank line between groups. LinkedIn strips formatting, so white space is the only structure.
4. The turn: what most people get wrong, in one or two lines.
5. The action: what to do this week, concrete.
6. The call to action, one line, plain words, no link.

Writing notes
- Lines of 1 to 2 sentences. Never a paragraph of 4 lines on mobile.
- Say the credential once, as fact, where it earns the claim. "I coach the Australian men's water polo team" in the body, not in the first line.
- No "Agree?" or "Thoughts?" endings. Ask one real question only if Marin wants replies on a real point.
- Can end on the action rather than the call to action if the piece is better for it.

Image
- Optional. If used: one of Marin's black and white photos, or a plain ivory card with the hook in DM Sans. Describe it in one line in the header block.

File header block
```
Channel: LinkedIn post
Paste into: LinkedIn, new post
Attach: <image note or "none">
Check before posting: first 3 lines read as a complete thought on mobile
First comment: <the link line>
```
