# Substack newsletter

Where it goes: Marin's Substack, new post, sent as email and published on the web.

Limits
- 700 to 1,200 words. People read it in email. Respect that.
- Subject line under 50 characters, sentence case. The subject is the first line of the idea, not a tease.
- Subtitle (shows under the title and as the email preview) under 120 characters. It adds the second half of the thought, it does not repeat the subject.

Shape
1. Open with a short scene or the claim. Two to four sentences. Email readers decide in the first screen.
2. The mechanism, in plain prose. This is where Substack earns its length: explain properly, with the facts on record.
3. One section heading at most, H2 sentence case, if the piece turns.
4. What to do this week. Prose or a short list of 3 to 5 lines.
5. Sign off: one or two lines, warm, Marin's name. "Marin" on its own line.
6. The call to action after the sign off, one line with the link, as a PS or a button label in sentence case.

Writing notes
- This is the closest channel to a letter. Write to one reader. Contractions are fine.
- It can carry one more idea than the post, but still one claim.
- Say what Marin is doing this week with the team if it is on record and relevant. Readers subscribe for the inside view.
- No "Welcome to this week's edition". Start.
- A short "Also this week" block of two or three links to Insights articles is allowed at the end.

File header block
```
Channel: Substack newsletter
Paste into: Substack, new post
Subject: <under 50 characters>
Subtitle: <under 120 characters>
Attach: header image optional, <note>
Check before sending: subject and subtitle read as one thought; send a test email and read it on a phone
```
