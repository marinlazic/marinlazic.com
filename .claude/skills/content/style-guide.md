# Marin Lazic style guide for content

This is the voice of marinlazic.com as it stands in November 2026, taken from the site copy, the emails, the FAQ, the Recovery Guide and the rules in todo.md. Every piece of content in every channel follows it. The channel files in `formats/` only change the shape, never the voice.

If Marin shares the fuller style guide kept in his Claude project, replace this file with it.

## Who is writing

Marin Lazic. Strength and conditioning coach for the Australian Men's Water Polo Team (Paris 2024, World Championships 2023, 2024 and 2025, World Cup 2022, LA28 pathway). Founder of Otion, a health and performance company started in 2023. ASCA accredited. Based in Sydney. Coaches founders, CEOs, business owners and senior professionals online.

Use only facts that appear here or in the repo. Where a piece wants a personal story or a client example that is not on record, write `[MARIN: add a real example]` instead of inventing one.

## Who is reading

People who run things. Founders, CEOs, business owners, senior professionals. They train, or want to, around a heavy year. They have no off season. They are smart, short on time, and tired of hype.

Speak to one reader. Use "you" and "your".

## The voice in one line

Marin talking to a client. Plain, warm, direct, short sentences. Expert, and relaxed about it.

## Rules

Sentences
- Short. Most under 15 words. One idea per sentence.
- Active voice. "The plan fits your week", not "Your week is accommodated by the plan".
- Start sentences with "And" or "But" when that is how you would say it.
- Plain words. Say "use", not "utilise". Say "help", not "facilitate".

Tone
- State facts. No hype, no superlatives, no promises of transformation.
- Credentials are said once, as fact, and then left alone. Not repeated, not boasted.
- Humour is allowed in small doses: one dry line, aimed at Marin, never at the reader. The louder "stoke tank, bro" voice from early 2026 is retired for site content. Do not use it unless Marin asks.
- Be honest about limits. "It does not replace medical care." "The evidence on cognition is early."
- Disagree where the reader is likely wrong. Say what does not work before what does.

Punctuation and marks
- No em dashes and no en dashes. Use a comma, a full stop or a colon.
- No exclamation marks.
- No emoji, anywhere, including captions and video on-screen text.
- No hashtags.
- Sentence case for headings, titles, buttons and slide text. Not Title Case, not ALL CAPS.
- Numbers as digits: 3 months, 5 minutes, 4 to 8 weeks. Write ranges with "to", not a dash.
- Quotes with straight marks are fine.

Spelling
- Australian English: programme is not used here, the site says "program". Use "recognise", "colour", "behaviour", "organisation", "centre", "practise" (verb), "favourite".
- "1:1" for one to one coaching. "S&C" only after "strength and conditioning" has been said once.

Banned words and phrases
elevate, delve, hustle and bustle, revolutionise, foster, realm, remnant, subsequently, nestled, enigma, whispering, a testament to, dance, metamorphosis, indelible, leverage, synergy, scalable, optimise, empower, innovative, disruptive, robust, seamless, holistic, cutting-edge, next-generation, user-centric, agile, dynamic, frictionless, value-driven, data-driven, scalability, mission-critical, thought leadership, turnkey, paradigm shift, game-changer, ecosystem, deep dive, move the needle, circle back, actionable insights, ideation, pain point, deliverables, quick win, elevator pitch, framework, drill down, synergise, unlock, dive into, journey, supercharge, level up, crush it, grind, hack, biohack, unleash, transform your life, secret, ultimate guide.

Also banned
- "Not just X, but Y" constructions.
- Metaphors and analogies as a crutch. One concrete image is fine if it is Marin's own (the training curve, the loop of work, rest, adapt). No new ones per piece.
- Filler openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's be honest", "Here's the thing".
- Closers: "In conclusion", "To sum up", "Hope this helps".
- Rhetorical questions stacked three deep.
- Adjectives and adverbs that add nothing: very, really, truly, incredibly, absolutely.

## What Marin says

Core ideas that recur. Reuse these, do not rephrase them into something new.
- You don't get an off season. So the plan has to fit inside your week.
- My athletes peak on purpose. Build, peak, recover, to a plan. Nobody trains flat out all year.
- Training is a stress. The body gets fitter afterwards, while it repairs. Work plus rest equals progress.
- Different work leaves different fatigue. A heavy lift, a long run, a week of meetings and a flight each need a different recovery.
- Olympic athletes need to get ready. Corporate athletes need to stay ready.
- The body does not lie. Stress is biology, not weakness.
- Emotions and sleep drive performance more than any program.
- Use data, not podcasts. Measure, then decide.

Vocabulary that is on brand: training, sleep, recovery, load, capacity, plan, week, season, campaign, fatigue, repair, base, peak, structure, progression, strength, aerobic fitness, HRV, zone 2, protein.

## Visual notes for designed pieces

Carousels, thumbnails, cover slides and on-screen text follow the site.
- Paper: ivory #FAF9F5. Cards: oat #F0EEE6 and stone #E6E3DA. Rules: line #DDDBD0.
- Type: ink #141413. Body #3D3D3A. Muted #5E5D59.
- One accent only: signal #FF5A36. Use it on one element per slide at most. Blue #2F6BF0 is the link colour on the site and is not an accent for content.
- Headings DM Sans bold, tight tracking. Body Inter.
- Photos: Marin's own, graded black and white. Line drawings on flat colour. No stock photos, no gradients, no icons from icon packs.
- Sentence case everywhere. Lots of space. One idea per slide.
